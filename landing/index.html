the problem

The first week is silent.

Nobody explains the codebase. You open file after file, guess at the names, and slowly piece together something nobody ever wrote down.

You are guessing

Names are abbreviated, the important logic is in three random folders, and nothing tells you where to start.

Asking costs too much

You do not want to keep interrupting the one person who knows. So you keep guessing.

Generic answers do not help

Explaining one file in isolation ignores the other twenty-nine. You already know how to read a function.

the solution

Point it at a folder. Get back four things.

One pass over your code, then everything after that is instant — because the understanding is already saved.

01

A report worth reading

One interactive page with the project overview, the file map, the risky spots and what to look at first. It opens in your browser and works offline.

02

Any file, in plain English

Ask about one file and get what it is for, what it depends on, and why it exists — without reading all of it first.

03

Know what breaks

Point at a file before you change it and see who depends on it. No more finding out in production.

04

Ask questions like a person

"Where is auth handled?" gets an answer from your code, not from the model's memory. Follow-ups keep the context.

how it works

Four steps, and you only do the first one.

It reads your project in several passes rather than one, because a single pass over a whole repository gives confident nonsense.

  1. You point at a folder

    That is the whole setup. No config file, no key, no choosing a model.

  2. It reads everything worth reading

    Skips binaries and build output, keeps the interesting parts of very long files, and works through the rest in batches that fit.

  3. It works out how the pieces fit

    What depends on what, where the risky code is, which files everything else leans on.

  4. It saves the understanding

    Your report, file explanations and chat all read from the same saved result. Ask a fifth question as fast as the first.

the impact

Your first day becomes your first coffee break.

The goal was never to write code for you. It is to get you from "I have no idea where to start" to making a real change.

Stop reading at random

You get an order to read things in, so you stop opening files hoping one of them is the important one.

Ask without awkwardness

The person who knows the codebase is not interrupted, and you are not guessing quietly for a week.

Change things more safely

See what depends on a file before you touch it, not after something falls over.

the hackathon

Byakugan was built during the IBM Bob 2.0 Hackathon. Bob was the reason we got to a working demo at all.

Building

Finishing

Two developers, one weekend, and a tool we now use on codebases we did not write.
